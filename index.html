SCENE 01 / UNDERSTANDING LIGHT

What is light?

Light is electromagnetic radiation. Its wavelength is related to its colour and affects how it interacts with materials.

Visible light spectrum

Red light has a longer wavelength than blue or violet light. White light contains a range of visible wavelengths.

KEY IDEA

To investigate optical cloaking, we must understand how light travels and interacts with materials.

SCIENCE STATUS: Established principles of light. The diagram is illustrative.

SCENE 03 / Engineering the Direction of Light

A metasurface contains engineered subwavelength structures. By designing their optical response across a surface, engineers can create a spatial phase gradient that changes the direction of a transmitted or reflected wave.

INTERACTIVE WAVEFRONT DEMONSTRATION

The animated dots indicate wave travel. The outgoing lines illustrate a possible designed direction, not a calculated electromagnetic field.

What changes at the surface?

The engineered structures impose different phase delays at different positions. Under suitable design conditions, this phase gradient can steer the outgoing wavefront.

Why this matters for SACM

SACM proposes distributing optical control across many segments. However, steering a wave at a surface does not by itself create a cloak. A cloak must manage the field around a target and produce the intended field outside it.

SCIENCE STATUS: Phase-gradient metasurfaces are an established research approach. This animation is illustrative, not a numerical simulation or proof of SACM performance.

SCENE 04 / Real Science. Real Evidence.

Invisibility cloaking is a real field of electromagnetic research. However, every experimental result has specific limits.

2006 | Engineered metamaterial

Wave type: Microwaves

Hidden target: Copper cylinder

Mechanism: Engineered material structures altered electromagnetic-wave propagation around the target.

Observed result: Reduced scattering and shadow over a limited operating frequency range.

2011 | Visible-light cloak

Wave type: Visible light

Material: Two calcite crystals

Hidden target: A tiny object in a controlled optical arrangement

Observed result: The object was concealed under the demonstrated viewing and illumination conditions.

Open the 2011 scientific paper ↗

What has NOT been proven?

These experiments do not demonstrate a person becoming invisible in ordinary daylight from every viewing angle. The 2006 device operated with microwaves, while the 2011 calcite cloak used a specific, carefully controlled optical arrangement.

How SACM relates to this research

SACM proposes investigating segmented optical control for a human-sized target. It is a conceptual research model inspired by real optical principles, not a tested invisibility device.

EVIDENCE STANDARD: Published experimental results are distinct from the proposed SACM design. No SACM cloaking performance has yet been measured or demonstrated.
